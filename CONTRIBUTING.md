# Contributing to Open Research

Anyone may contribute. No degree, affiliation or prior publication is required. We evaluate the work, not the credentials of its author.

## Getting started
- Propose a new problem or domain through Issues, or discuss an existing project with its maintainers.
- Fork the repository, make your changes and submit a pull request. Existing research may be submitted directly with sufficient context.
- For a new project, choose one primary domain and a descriptive kebab-case folder name. Cross-link related fields instead of duplicating projects.
- Use the minimum [project template](templates/project/).

## Minimum project requirements
Each project contains README.md, AUTHORS.md, archive/ and code/. The README identifies the problem, context and prior work, results, methods, limitations, research status, verification status, AI assistance and how to check the work. If code is not applicable, explain why and retain the directory with a placeholder. All other organization is at the maintainers' discretion. Use descriptive names and preserve meaningful historical checkpoints in archive/; avoid redundant large binaries, credentials and restricted data.

## Quality and review
Submissions must be coherent, original or properly attributed, and sufficiently documented to enable meaningful scrutiny. Support claims with derivations, evidence, reproducible procedures or appropriate references. Check results and assumptions before submission, using multiple substantive checks where applicable. Disclose AI assistance, including its role and the checks performed; authors remain responsible for every claim. A polished AI-generated document alone is not evidence.

Proposals and partial results are welcome when explicitly identified. Unresolved conjectures must not be presented as established facts. Independent verification is encouraged but not a prerequisite for admission; see [verification standards](VERIFICATION.md). Reviewers may request revisions or decline submissions that do not meet these standards.

## Credit and rights
Identify authors, substantive contributors, maintainers and prior work. Cite the research you build on and credit new contributions accurately. Git history supplements, rather than replaces, scholarly attribution. Agree on authorship for joint publications based on actual contributions; do not equate every PR with paper authorship. Cite or acknowledge Open Research where it materially supported the work. Contributors retain copyright and grant the public the permissions in our [licensing policy](LICENSE.md). Only submit material you have permission to release.

Corrections and negative results are valuable contributions. Discuss disagreements with evidence and follow the [code of conduct](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).
